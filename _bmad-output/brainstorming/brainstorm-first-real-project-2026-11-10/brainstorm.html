Where it started

The spark

Erfan came to the session as a fresh computer engineering graduate, now studying for an MSc in software engineering. He wanted a first real project to learn by doing. He already had an idea.

💡

The idea Erfan

Post an activity invite ("Anyone want a burger? Here are the details.") and friends just answer yes or no. No long group-chat discussion.

😟

The pain Erfan

People don't ask in the group chat. They are afraid to hear "no", they don't want the pressure of a chat, or they just want to know: does anyone really want to come?

🤝

After the yes Erfan

When people say yes, they arrange the meetup inside the app, or move to a messenger.

Technique 1

🎭 Role Playing: four masks

We put on four different "masks" and looked at the app through each person's eyes. Each mask is a chat: Erfan's ideas on the right, Mary's on the left.

Erfan idea or insight Mary idea dashed = open question
Mask 1
📱

The friend who gets the invite

"I want to answer fast, and I don't want to feel bad."

Mary Silent no. The inviter only sees who said yes. Nobody ever sees a "no".
Erfan InsightBut if ignoring means no, the inviter is back to waiting. That waiting is the tiring part we want to remove.
Mary Deadline. "Answer in 1 hour." After that, no answer becomes no automatically. The inviter never waits forever.
Erfan The invite pops up as a notification with Yes/No buttons. Answer without opening the app.
Erfan You can change your answer later inside the app.
Erfan A presence score: saying yes raises a score that shows you are an open, "present" person.
Erfan Could a score put pressure on people? That is the opposite of the app's goal.
Erfan InsightA friend who often says no may be short on money, and too shy to say it.
Erfan Friends who said yes can pay the share of a friend who can't afford it.
Erfan A fun status: "today I'm in the mood", "this week I'm short on money". Friends know without an awkward talk.
Erfan When friends see "low money", they suggest free activities: dinner at someone's house, shared food.
Erfan "Today's food is on me": the inviter treats everyone.
Erfan Are money and status features a good idea? Maybe they bring shame or awkwardness.
Mary Cost tag on every invite: Free / $ / $$ / $$$. Friends see the price before they answer. Nobody needs to explain money.
Mask 2
🥲

The inviter who gets zero yes

"I asked. Nobody came. Now what?"

Erfan Last chance reminder. 10 minutes before the deadline, the invite pops up again: "last chance to say yes".
Erfan If nobody comes, the inviter goes alone and shares a photo of the food. Friends see what they missed and want to join next time.
Erfan How does the inviter feel when everyone says no? Not sure yet.
Mary Soft result screen. Never show "0 people". Show "Nobody is free tonight" and a one-tap button to post the same invite for another day.
Erfan InsightIf someone hears "no" from everyone 5 times a month, maybe friends don't want them, or their activities don't interest the group.
Erfan Activity suggestions. The app learns which activities got the most yes in the group, and privately suggests similar ones. The user posts it as their own idea. Nobody knows it came from the app.
Mary InsightThis is the natural place for AI in the project, and it fits Erfan's goal to become an AI agentic engineer.
Mary Smart timing tip, private: "Your friends say yes most on Friday evenings."
Mask 3
🧑‍💼

The hiring manager

"Why should I care about this project?"

Erfan InsightA sharper problem: in a group chat everyone comments and changes the plan. The person who first suggested it ends up sad. The app keeps the plan as written.
Erfan What impresses: (1) a clear human problem, (2) features like AI recommendations and photo sharing, (3) a tech stack chosen for the AI-engineer goal.
Mary Locked plan + "make your own". Others can't edit or comment on an invite. Want a different plan? Post your own invite.
Mask 4
🔀

The friend who always changes plans

"Burger? What about sushi? And 9 is better..."

Erfan InsightLocked or flexible should be the inviter's choice. The inviter decides how much the plan can change.
Erfan Several options with the inviter's favourite: burger first, sushi also OK. If enough people (e.g. 3) prefer the other, it switches.
Erfan Time window instead of exact time (6–9 pm). Friends pick inside it.
Mary Auto-pick. The app looks at what the yes people chose and confirms the best: "Best time: 7:30, sushi wins".
Technique 2

🔄 Reverse Brainstorming: how could it fail?

We asked the opposite question: "How could this app fail, or even hurt people?" Then we flipped each danger into a feature that protects users. Left: the danger. Right: the flip.

Way to fail

Ghost members

Friends join but never send invites or answer. Nobody checks notifications or updates their status. The app goes quiet and dies. Erfan

↻
Flipped into
  • Shared photo album per outing. Everyone uploads there. No more "can you send me the photos?" A reason to open the app. Erfan
  • Mood status helps friends invite at the right moment: Sara is in the mood, Ali sends her a private invite. Erfan
  • The app suggests: "Sara is in a good mood tonight, maybe invite her to dinner." Erfan
  • Privately follow one person's status more closely (e.g. a crush). Erfan
  • Mutual secret crush: revealed only if BOTH people marked each other. Otherwise nobody ever knows. Mary

Mary asked: could following someone's mood feel like stalking? Erfan decided it is OK: Sara already shares her status with the group, and "follow" just sends Ali a notification instead of him checking by hand.

Way to fail

Too many features

Everything we discussed makes the screen confusing. Erfan

↻
Flipped into
  • Simple-first design. The UI must be extremely simple, and every feature easy to reach. Erfan
Way to fail

Notification spam

Friends get so many notifications that they mute the app. Mary

↻
Flipped into
  • Limits: max invites per day and quiet hours, so every notification feels worth opening. Mary
Way to fail

"Pay for a friend" is awkward

The friend may be shy to accept. Others may not want to pay. Erfan

↻
Flipped into (Mary's 4 ideas, Erfan chose 2)
  • Anonymous gift 🎁: the friend only sees "your share is covered", never who paid. Mary · kept
  • Pay me back later tracker: come now, pay back when you can. Mary · kept
  • Treat rotation: the app tracks whose turn it is to treat. Mary
  • No paying at all: cost tag + "budget mode". Mary
Way to fail

The exposed "no"

Someone says no to a friend in private chat, but the app shows they said yes to another plan. Drama. Also: people may not want others to see their yes or no at all. Erfan

↻
Flipped into
  • Answers are visible only to people inside that invite. No yes/no history for the group. Mary
  • You choose who sees your answer: only the inviter, all members, or hide it from specific people. Erfan
  • "Busy tonight" cover: people outside the invite only see a neutral label, never which plan you joined. Mary
Way to fail

Group drama

Part of a group wants to go out without one member who is still in the group. Erfan

↻
Flipped into
  • Personal (1-to-1) invites next to group invites. Erfan
  • Private guest list: the inviter picks exactly which members are invited. Erfan
  • Invisible invites: people not on the list never see the invite exists. Invitees see who is coming only after they say yes. Mary

The recipe

🍔 The v1 tech stack, as a burger

Mary proposed, Erfan asked good questions ("pros and cons?", "can SQLite handle photos?"), and Erfan agreed on each layer.

PWA web appinstallable, works on the phone, web push
HTMXserver HTML, mostly Python
SQLModelthe ORM between code and data
Python + FastAPIthe backend, the meat
SQLite → PostgreSQLSQLite locally · Neon or Supabase at deploy
🍟 Side dish, later: photo albumPhotos don't live in the database. Files go to storage (a local folder in dev; Supabase Storage, Cloudflare R2 or S3 in production). The DB only keeps the path. Erfan kept it in "Could".
🥤 Side dish, later: AIAI features through an LLM API in a later version.
🚚 Delivery (proposed)Host on Render, Railway or Fly.io. Careful: SQLite on free hosting can lose data on redeploy, so use managed PostgreSQL there.
Why not React?Most job demand, but JavaScript + two projects + a slower start. Maybe later.
Why not Streamlit?Python-only, but not good for a mobile app for normal users.
Why not Flutter / React Native?Real mobile, but a new language. Later.
Why not NoSQL?Firebase or Mongo fit worse: groups, invites and answers are relational data.

📌

Pillar 2: your plan is respected

The inviter chooses locked or flexible. The inviter picks the guest list. No more plans that get changed until the person who suggested them feels sad.

🤖

AI as a quiet, private coach

Suggestions that only you see: activities your group likes, the best time to ask. Never public. And it is the bridge to Erfan's goal: becoming an AI agentic engineer.

🔒

Privacy is the core, not an extra

Reverse brainstorming showed the app itself could create new drama (the exposed "no"). So privacy is not a feature on top. It is the foundation.
