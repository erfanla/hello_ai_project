# PRD Quality Review — Low-pressure friend invite app (prd-hello_ai_project-2026-11-10)

*Reviewed 2026-11-11 against `prd-validation-checklist.md`. Stakes: hobby / learning project, ~12 friend testers, 4-week test, ~2-page target. Rigor judged at that level.*

## Overall verdict
For a first project, this is a good PRD. It has a clear, real thesis (Silent No + Deadline, backed by the comparables gap in addendum §A), it is honest about scope, and it names counter-metrics. The weak spot is done-ness: the Invitation lifecycle has gaps that story writing will hit right away. Editing is allowed but never defined, nothing says where a Yes-sayer finds the Invitation after its Deadline, and the timing of the Yes List and Notes is unclear. Two of the four primary metrics also measure something the app cannot observe. All of these are small fixes, but they should be made before epics and stories.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The main decisions are written as decisions, not as "considerations": everyone must sign up (Glossary "User": "there are no guest answers"), Notes instead of live chat (§4.4, §6.2), no answer changes in v1 (§6.2 "Next (v1.1): change your Answer"). The Open Questions are really open (§8 Q1–Q3), and Q4 ("Do the friends really feel this problem? Not yet asked") is a brave, useful thing to admit. The weak part is that some trade-offs record what was chosen but not what was given up. And the strongest objection to the core rule is never raised.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Silent No can be inferred from the Yes List, and the PRD doesn't say so (§1 Core rules, §3 "Silent No", FR-9). The rule says "Nobody is shown a 'no'". But in a Group of 6, an Inviter who sees a Yes List of 1 knows that 5 people said No or didn't answer. An Invitee who said Yes can count the same way. This is the first objection a skeptical friend will raise, and the PRD doesn't address it. The real promise is "no *individual* No is shown", and that is weaker than §1 suggests. *Fix:* Add one sentence to FR-9 or §1 that states the limit. For example: "Silent No hides who said No, not how many people were asked; with small Guest Lists, absence can be inferred. Accepted for v1." Or add it as an Open Question if it is not yet decided.
- **[medium]** The required sign-up drops a known advantage without saying so (Glossary "User"; FR-1; addendum §A Takeaway 1). The addendum notes that comparable apps let friends "answer from a link with no install or account". The PRD chose the opposite, but its reason ("notifications and Silent No need known users") is only in `.memlog.md`. A reader of the PRD sees the decision without the trade-off, and the sign-up friction risk recorded in the memlog doesn't appear anywhere in the PRD. *Fix:* Add a one-line rationale under FR-1 (what was given up and why). Also link it to the join-rate metric suggested under Strategic coherence.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is very little furniture here. The Vision could not be swapped into another invite app, because it names the specific bet ("saying no never needs a reason", "Your plan stays your plan"). The two personas (Soroush, Ali) each drive real requirements: UJ-1 led to FR-6 Open Invitations, and UJ-2 led to the iPhone fallback in FR-8. The three NFRs are short, and two of them have numbers. The addendum has real comparables with an honest source warning ("Many sources are vendor or SEO pages"). There is no innovation theater.

### Findings
- **[low]** The "silent invitation" promised in the Vision is never delivered (§1 ¶2, §2.1 "Emotional" JTBD). The Vision says "sometimes a **silent invitation** is the simplest way to say 'let's just be together'". The bold text makes it look like a product concept. But it isn't in the Glossary and no FR delivers it, and the name is close enough to "Silent No" to confuse readers. *Fix:* Either say in plain words that an ordinary Invitation *is* the silent invitation (because no talk is needed), or drop the bold so it doesn't read as a feature.
- **[low]** One NFR is an adjective, not a limit (§4 Cross-cutting NFRs, "every screen works well on a phone browser"). *Fix:* Give it a limit, for example "usable at 360px width with no horizontal scroll; tap targets ≥ 44px".

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the features follow it. Answering is one tap (FR-8), No answers are hidden (FR-9), the Deadline ends the waiting (FR-10), and nobody else can change the plan (FR-7). The non-goals (§5) protect the thesis: "No scores, ratings… (they create pressure)". The success metrics form a funnel, with SM-4 (repeat use) marked ⭐ to match addendum Takeaway 3. Each counter-metric is paired with the metric it balances, and the parked growth ideas are explicitly checked against SM-C1 (§6.2). That is strong practice. The problems are in *measurement*, not in direction.

### Findings
- **[high]** SM-3 and SM-4 measure events the app cannot see (§7 SM-3 "Invitations that end in a real meetup", SM-4 "Users who went to a meetup"). No FR records whether a meetup happened or who attended. The app only knows the Yes List. As written, the two most important metrics (including the ⭐ one) cannot be computed, or they will quietly become "Yes List not empty", which is a different thing. *Fix:* State the data source. Either (a) define the proxies, e.g. "meetup = Invitation not Cancelled with Yes List ≥ 1" and "went = on Yes List", or (b) say they come from a manual check-in with the friends at the end of each week. Option (b) is fine at this scale, but it must be written down.
- **[medium]** The riskiest assumption has no metric (§8 Q4; memlog sign-up friction risk). Two risks could sink the test before any Invitation exists: friends may not finish joining through the Group Link, and they may not feel the problem at all. SM-1 to SM-4 only start counting after people have joined. *Fix:* Add a simple activation metric, e.g. "SM-0: invited friends who join a Group within 48 h: at least 80%". Also note that Q4 is answered by a pre-build message, not by a metric.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Many FRs have clear, testable consequences. FR-5 ("never see the Invitation or any notification about it"), FR-8 ("There is no field for a reason"), FR-10 ("never '0 people'") and FR-13 (four precise bullets) are all good. But the Invitation lifecycle, which every epic will touch, has holes. An engineer writing stories would have to guess in several places.

### Findings
- **[high]** Editing an Invitation is allowed but never specified (FR-7 "Only the Inviter can edit an Invitation"). No FR says which fields can be edited, whether editing is possible after the Deadline, or what happens to existing Yes Answers when the time or place changes. It also doesn't say whether Invitees are notified, or whether someone who said Yes to "8pm burger" stays on the Yes List for "10pm pizza". This conflicts with the spirit of "Your plan stays your plan": people said Yes to a specific plan. *Fix:* Add an FR "Edit an Invitation" that lists what can be edited and when, and says what happens to the Yes List and notifications. Or, simplest for v1, declare `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` editing and point to FR-13 (cancel and recreate).
- **[high]** Nothing says where a Yes-sayer finds the Invitation after the Deadline (FR-6, Glossary "Open Invitation", FR-11, FR-13). Open Invitations disappear when the Deadline passes, but UJ-1's resolution ("the people on the Yes List use Notes… and meet at the shop") and FR-13 ("so the Yes List can still read its Notes") both need the Invitation to stay reachable after that. No screen or FR covers "my upcoming / past Invitations", so the climax of UJ-1 has no home. *Fix:* Add a bullet to FR-6, or a small new FR: "A User can see Invitations they created or answered Yes to, including closed and Cancelled ones, until [N hours after 'when']."
- **[medium]** The timing of the Yes List and of Notes is unclear (FR-9, FR-10, FR-11, §4.4 Description). Can the Inviter watch the Yes List grow before the Deadline, or only see it "when the Deadline ends" (UJ-1 Climax)? Can Notes be used before the Deadline? FR-11 doesn't say, but §4.4 says "After the plan is settled". These choices change how the UI and the rules (permissions) work. *Fix:* Add one bullet each to FR-9 ("Inviter sees the Yes List live / only after Deadline") and FR-11 ("Notes open as soon as a User is on the Yes List / only after Deadline").
- **[medium]** Answers being final in v1 is only implied (FR-8; §6.2 "Next (v1.1): change your Answer"). An engineer reading FR-8 alone won't know that a Yes cannot be undone. That matters for Ali, who said Yes and then can't come, and for SM-3. *Fix:* Add a bullet to FR-8: "In v1 an Answer is final; to drop out, use Notes."
- **[medium]** On iPhone, notifications only work after installing to the home screen, and the PRD doesn't say so (FR-12 "Users *can* install…", UJ-3, FR-1 "under 1 minute", addendum §B). On iOS, Web Push only works for a PWA added to the home screen. So for iPhone users, installing is required for UJ-2, not optional. It also has to fit inside the "under 1 minute" onboarding in UJ-3. *Fix:* In FR-12, state that installing is required for notifications on iPhone, and that onboarding guides the user through it. Also say whether the 1-minute limit includes the install.
- **[low]** Small limits are missing (FR-11 "short Notes"; FR-4 Deadline vs "when"). There is no length for Notes, and nothing stops a Deadline that ends after the event's start time. *Fix:* Give a number for "short" (e.g. ≤ 280 chars), and add "Deadline must end before 'when'" (or allow it explicitly).

## Scope honesty — strong
This is the best section. §5 Non-Goals does real work (no chat, no money, no native app). §6.2 separates "Next (v1.1)", "Later" and "Parked growth ideas", and gives a reason for parking them ("they risk adding pressure"). FR-4 marks "voting on options is out of scope" inline. All six inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed, and the number of open items (4 Open Questions plus 7 assumptions) is fine for a friend test. The one quiet omission is minor.

### Findings
- **[low]** The lifecycle of a Group membership is never mentioned (§4.1). Nothing says whether a Member can leave a Group, whether anyone can remove a Member, whether a Group Link can expire or be reset, or whether a User can delete their account. At 12 friends this is probably fine to skip, but say so, so stories don't invent it. *Fix:* Add one line to §5 or §6.2: "No leaving/removing Members, link reset, or account deletion in v1."

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD is chain-top: it feeds architecture and then epics and stories (§0). The Glossary is real and mostly followed. The FR, UJ and SM IDs are unique, and the cross-references resolve ("see §6.2", "Open Question 1", "SM-C1"). Each UJ has a named protagonist with context. The main downstream risk is that the Invitation has states the Glossary doesn't name.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The Invitation states are not modelled (Glossary "Open Invitation"; FR-10; FR-13 "marked **Cancelled**"). The PRD uses three states (open, closed after the Deadline, Cancelled), but only "Open Invitation" is defined, and "Cancelled" appears only in bold inside FR-13. Architecture will need a clear state list for the data model and the rules (permissions). *Fix:* Add a Glossary entry, "Invitation status: Open → Closed (Deadline passed) | Cancelled (by Inviter)", and use those words in FR-6, FR-10, FR-11 and FR-13.
- **[low]** In UJ-1, the wrong group gets the notification (UJ-1 "Members get a notification and answer"). FR-5 and FR-12 say that only *Invitees* (Members on the Guest List) are notified. *Fix:* Change "Members" to "Invitees" in UJ-1.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a consumer social app with real UX stakes, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing. The three UJs are the right number: create, answer and join map to the three core flows. The FRs are short, and the technical choices are correctly moved to addendum §B. At about 3 pages, the PRD is close to the 2-page target without losing anything it needs. It is not over-formalized: there is no persona list, no market sizing and no compliance padding.

## Mechanical notes
- **FR order:** FR-13 sits between FR-7 and FR-8 (§4.2), so IDs are unique but not in reading order. That's fine, since IDs should stay stable, but consider a note "(added later)" or moving it after FR-12 in the next revision.
- **UJ traceability:** FR-2, FR-7 and FR-11 have no "Realizes UJ-x" line, while the other FRs do. FR-13 uses "Relates to" while the others use "Realizes". FR-11 clearly realizes UJ-1 (Resolution).
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** There are 6 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and 7 index entries. The extra entry (§8 Q4, "friends share the pain") has no inline tag. That's acceptable because it lives in Open Questions, but either tag it inline or label the index entry "(from §8)". SM-C2's tag says "number proposed by John". A future reader won't know who John is (the PM agent), so write "proposed during PRD interview, to confirm".
- **Small number drift:** UJ-2 says "done in 3 seconds", but the Speed NFR says "under 5 seconds". Not a conflict, but pick one number as the target.
- **Privacy NFR wording:** "Invitations they are on the Guest List of" leaves out the Inviter's own Invitations (the Inviter is not an Invitee). Add "or created".
- **Required sections:** All present for the stakes (Vision, users and UJs, Glossary, FRs, NFRs, Non-Goals, Scope, SMs with counter-metrics, Open Questions, Assumptions Index).
